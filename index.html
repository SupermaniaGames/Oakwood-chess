            How to play chess

            Chess is a game for two players. Each side starts with 16 pieces, and the goal is to trap the other player's king so it can't escape — that's called checkmate.

            Pawn

            Moves straight ahead one square (two squares on its very first move), but it can only capture diagonally, one square ahead. It's the only piece that captures differently than it moves.

            Knight

            Moves in an "L" shape: two squares in one direction, then one square sideways. It's the only piece that can jump right over other pieces.

            Bishop

            Slides diagonally, as far as it wants. Each bishop stays on the same color square for the whole game — one starts on light squares, one on dark.

            Rook

            Slides in a straight line — up, down, left, or right — as far as it wants. Rooks are also part of a special move called castling (below).

            Queen

            The most powerful piece — combines the rook and bishop, sliding any direction (straight or diagonal) as far as it wants.

            King

            Moves only one square in any direction. It's the piece you have to protect — if it's trapped, the game is over.

            Special moves

            Castling: if the king and one of its rooks haven't moved yet, and nothing's between them, they can move together in one turn — the king hops two squares toward the rook, tucking itself somewhere safer.

            En passant: a special pawn capture. If an opponent's pawn moves two squares and lands right beside yours, you can capture it as if it had only moved one square — but only immediately, on your very next move.

            Promotion: if a pawn makes it all the way to the far end of the board, it transforms into any other piece you choose — almost everyone picks a queen.

            Winning the game

            You win by putting the opponent's king in checkmate: it's under attack, and there's no move that gets it out of trouble. If nobody can make progress, the game can end in a draw instead — nobody wins.
